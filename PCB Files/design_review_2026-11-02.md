### 1 & 2 — J1 hole clearance and annular ring

All 44 errors are J1, every zone against every layer, consistently 0.208 mm vs the 0.250 mm
`min_hole_clearance` rule — a 0.042 mm shortfall.

This is a **regression, not a pre-existing condition**. Running DRC on the committed board (`aa4d02f`) with
its own `.kicad_pro` rules alongside gives **0 errors**. J1's position, its pad definitions, and the board
outline are byte-identical between the two. The only thing that differs is the zone fill geometry — the GND
pour on F.Cu/In1.Cu went from 7 islands / 11094 points to 11 islands / 14357 points. The zones were refilled
after F1 and D14 went in, and the new fill reaches into J1 where the old one did not.

The underlying cause is finding 2: at a 0.10 mm annular ring there is almost no copper margin around those
holes, so any pour that approaches J1 immediately violates hole clearance.

Pick one:

- **Enlarge J1's THT pads** to 0.75–0.80 mm on the 0.45 mm drill (ring 0.15–0.175 mm). Fixes both findings and
  gets you back inside IPC Class 2. Requires editing the `My_Components:JAE DX07S024XJ1R1100` footprint.
- Add a local clearance or a small keepout around J1 so the pours stay off it. Fixes finding 1 only.
- Relax `min_hole_clearance` to 0.20 mm. Fixes finding 1 on paper; confirm your fab accepts it, and finding 2
  stands regardless.

### 3 — D10 thermal

F1 (0685T3000-01, 3 A) now bounds the 12V_PROT branch, which is well matched to the SS34's 3 A rating — good
change. But the SS34 is in an SMA with roughly 75 °C/W to ambient on a typical pad. At 2 A continuous
(≈0.45 V forward) that is ~0.9 W and a ~67 °C rise; at the 3 A fuse limit, ~1.5 W and ~112 °C. Inside a
vehicle enclosure at 60–85 °C ambient, the 3 A case exceeds the 150 °C junction limit.

Also note 1206 fuses derate 15–20 % at 85 °C, so F1 carries nearer 2.4 A continuous when hot.

Either establish that the comma device's real draw stays near 1 A, or widen D10's thermal pads and add
copper, or move to a larger package.

### 6 — Relay, continuously energised

From the G5V-2 datasheet, page 4, "Correct Use → Long-term Continuously ON Contacts":

> Using the Relay in a circuit where the Relay will be ON continuously for long periods (without switching)
> can lead to unstable contacts because the heat generated by the coil itself can affect the insulation,
> causing a film to develop on the contact surfaces.

That is precisely this design — the coil is energised the whole time the box is powered. Omron's own advice is
to use a fail-safe circuit design, which you do have (de-energised = stock passthrough), so the consequence of
contact degradation is degraded intercept rather than a dead car. Worth knowing, and worth periodically
exercising the relay in firmware if that is an option.

## What is verified correct

### Relay contact assignment — datasheet-verified

This is the safety-critical one, and it checks out. From the G5V-2 datasheet page 4, "Terminal
Arrangement/Internal Connections (Bottom View)", read off the internal connection diagram: the common
contacts route up/over to the armature pivot, so **COM = 4 and 13**, **NC = 6 and 11**, **NO = 8 and 9**.
Coil on 1 and 16.

| K1 pin | Role (datasheet) | Net | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | COM | `ADAS_CAN_L` | vehicle |
| 6 | NC | `ADAS_CAN_L_MOD` | ECU |
| 8 | NO | `ADAS_CAN_L_C` | comma |
| 13 | COM | `ADAS_CAN_H` | vehicle |
| 11 | NC | `ADAS_CAN_H_MOD` | ECU |
| 9 | NO | `ADAS_CAN_H_C` | comma |

De-energised → vehicle ↔ ECU. Energised → vehicle ↔ comma, with the ECU still hard-wired to the comma's
`_MOD` port. The fail-safe passthrough is wired the right way round.

### DG419LE — datasheet-verified

Fitted part `DG419LEDY-T1-GE4` matches the Vishay DG417LE/DG418LE/DG419LE datasheet exactly.

- Pinout (SOIC-8, page 1): 1=COM, 2=NC, 3=GND, 4=V+, 5=VL, 6=IN, 7=V-, 8=NO — matches schematic and board.
- Truth table (page 1): logic 0 → NC on. So `SBU 1` low (the R2+R3 240 Ω pulldown default) bridges `_C` to
  `_MOD`; logic 1 opens the bridge and switches R4 across the ECU pair. Correct.
- Supply +3 V to +16 V, abs max 18 V (page 2). `SW_VCC` sits near 11.9 V. Fine.
- "Control logic input can be over V+" (page 1) — a 3.3 V SBU1 against a 12 V rail is explicitly supported.
- Supply current (page 3): I+ and IL are 5 µA max each over full temperature. Two devices = 20 µA worst case
  through R6 = **66 mV** drop. R6 at 3.3 kΩ is comfortably sized.

### The SW_VCC clamp works

`12V_PROT` → R5 (160 Ω) → `VIN` → D12 (DSK14) → `SW_IN` → R6 (3.3 kΩ) → `SW_VCC`, clamped by D13
(BZT52C12, 12 V) and decoupled by C3.

Worked through against an SMAJ16A load-dump clamp at 26 V: `VIN` would reach ~22 V, R6 limits the Zener to
~3.1 mA, D13 dissipates ~37 mW against a 410 mW SOD-123 rating, and the DG419s stay at 12 V — well under
their 18 V absolute maximum. This is a well-built protection network and a clear improvement on hanging the
switches directly off the coil rail.

### PESD1CAN — datasheet-verified

VRWM 24 V, VBR 25.4–30.3 V, Cd 11 pF typ / 17 pF max, ΔCd/Cd = 0.1 %. Correct choice for automotive CAN
(tolerates bus-to-battery faults) and low enough capacitance for CAN FD. D1 has been moved onto the comma-side
`_C` pair, which is a better use than the dead `COMMA_P/N` pair it was on previously — all three ADAS CAN
segments plus the MRR tap are now covered.

## Component summary

28 schematic symbols, 33 footprints (the extra five are H1, H2, FID1–3). 19 unique parts. 73 nets.
**MPN coverage is 28/28** — every part is sourceable. 25 SMD, 6 THT.

Changes since the committed board (`aa4d02f`): **F1** added (0685T3000-01, 3 A, 1206) in series between
`Harness_12v` and D10's anode on a new `VBAT_FUSED` net, and **D14** added (PESD24VS1UB, SOD-523) from
`SBU 1` to GND.

F1's placement is right: it fuses only the branch that feeds the board and the comma device, leaving the
J3.1 → J4.1 passthrough to the ECU unfused, so a fault on the board cannot interrupt the ECU's supply.

D14 on `SBU 1` is a sensible addition, but note PESD24VS1UB has a **24 V** standoff on what is a 3.3 V logic
line. It protects the DG419 IN pins against large transients but provides essentially no clamping in the
0–24 V range. A 5 V part would clamp a 3.3 V line far better; 24 V is only the right choice if you expect
SBU1 to be pulled toward the 12 V domain in a fault.

## PCB layout

89.0 × 72.2 mm, 4 layers, 1.62 mm. 357 track segments (199 F.Cu, 158 B.Cu), 64 vias, 4 zones, routing complete.

| Layer | Use | Dielectric to next |
|---|---|---|
| F.Cu | signal + GND pour | 0.2103 mm prepreg |
| In1.Cu | GND plane | 1.04 mm core |
| In2.Cu | split power — 12V_PROT (priority 1) over VIN | 0.2103 mm prepreg |
| B.Cu | signal + GND pour | — |

**This stackup is the main structural weakness (finding 4).** F.Cu signals sit 0.21 mm above a solid ground
plane — excellent. B.Cu signals sit 0.21 mm below a *split power* plane, so their nearest reference is
discontinuous. The EMC analyzer raises 17 high and 11 warning `GP-001` reference-plane-gap findings and 19
`RP-001` missing-stitching-via-at-layer-transition findings, which is the expected consequence. With 158
segments on B.Cu this is not a corner case.

At CAN and CAN FD rates this will not stop the board working. It is an emissions and susceptibility risk in a
vehicle, which matters more here than it would on a bench board. The cheap mitigations are to add ground
stitching vias along the B.Cu routes (VS-002 reports 50 % of the board lacks ground vias) and to keep B.Cu
runs over the GND pour rather than crossing the VIN/12V_PROT split.

### Power routing

| Net | Length | Widths |
|---|---|---|
| `VBAT_FUSED` | 47.1 mm | 1.2 mm |
| `12V_PROT` | 20.3 mm | 0.25–1.0 mm |
| `VIN` | 15.6 mm | 0.5 mm |
| `GND` | 22.0 mm | 0.25–0.4 mm (rest is plane) |

The 0.2 mm segment that used to be on `12V_PROT` is gone; minimum is now 0.25 mm. At the 3 A fuse limit,
1.2 mm of 1 oz external copper is about a 20 °C rise by IPC-2221 — acceptable. The 0.25 mm stretch on
`12V_PROT` is backed by the In2.Cu pour, but it is worth widening for consistency.

### Differential pairs

Not length-matched. Deltas, measured from routed track lengths:

| Pair | H | L | Δ | Δ% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CMRR_FR | 36.00 | 30.92 | 5.08 | 14.1 % |
| CMRR_RR | 26.13 | 26.54 | 0.41 | 1.5 % |
| CMRR_FL | 39.88 | 34.51 | 5.37 | 13.5 % |
| CMRR_RL | 30.90 | 35.22 | 4.32 | 12.3 % |
| MRR | 98.14 | 93.43 | 4.70 | 4.8 % |
| ADAS (vehicle) | 16.81 | 25.17 | 8.36 | 33.2 % |
| ADAS (_C) | 24.09 | 26.15 | 2.06 | 7.9 % |
| ADAS (_MOD) | 71.17 | 50.14 | 21.03 | 29.6 % |
| DMS_LED1 | 44.67 | 40.30 | 4.38 | 9.8 % |

**This is not a functional problem.** The worst case, 21 mm, is about 140 ps of skew in FR4. A 500 kbps CAN
bit is 2 µs and a 5 Mbps CAN FD bit is 200 ns, so even the worst pair is under 0.1 % of a bit. The reason to
care is mode conversion — differential-to-common-mode leakage drives radiated emissions — and that argues for
tightening the three worst pairs (ADAS `_MOD`, ADAS vehicle, CMRR_FL/FR) rather than chasing all of them.

### Series resistance through the analog switches

Worth being aware of: in the default powered state (`SBU 1` low), the vehicle↔ECU CAN path runs through
**both** DG419s. The datasheet gives R_DS(on) as 11 Ω typical, 14 Ω max at room, and **18 Ω max over the full
−40 to +85 °C range**, so the differential loop picks up 22–36 Ω.

Against a 60 Ω effective bus load that is a significant divider — a 2 V differential drive could arrive near
1.25 V at the far receiver, against a ~0.9 V dominant threshold. It will probably work, but the margin is
thinner than it looks, and it is worst when cold.

Two mitigations worth considering: bench-test this mode at −40 °C if you can, and note that the full-intercept
mode (`SBU 1` high) takes the switches out of the signal path entirely, so it does not suffer this.

When `SBU 1` is high, R4's termination is 120 Ω **plus** one R_DS(on), so 131–138 Ω. CAN wants 120 Ω ±10 %
(108–132 Ω), so the hot end of that range is marginally out of spec. A 100–110 Ω R4 would centre it.

### Other layout findings

- **PM-002 ×7** — D1 at 0.37 mm and D10 at 0.35 mm from the board edge (recommend ≥1.0 mm); J1, J3, J4, J5, J6
  courtyards overhang the outline by 1.1–3.0 mm. The connector overhangs are almost certainly intentional
  (edge-mounted housings) — confirm and ignore. The two diodes are worth nudging inward.
- **ES-001** — D3 is far from connector J5. ESD current path should be short.
- **CG-AUD ×2** — J5 and J6 have **no ground pins at all** across 16 circuits. That is dictated by the Fisker
  CON1 pinout, not a choice you can make, but it means the four radar CAN pairs have no local return and
  depend entirely on the harness. Reinforces the stitching advice above.
- **TE-001** — no test points on any of 71 nets. For a board that sits inline in a vehicle harness,
  a few pads on `12V_PROT`, `SW_VCC`, `SBU 1` and the ADAS CAN pairs would make bring-up much easier.
- **DC-001** — C2 moderately far from U2.
- **BE-001** — `SW_VCC` routed near the board edge.

## False positives / reviewer overrides

- **PP-001 ×2 (high) — "U1.4 / U2.4 V+ has no DC path to a power rail"** — dismissed. There is a DC path:
  `12V_PROT` → R5 → `VIN` → D12 → `SW_IN` → R6 → `SW_VCC`. The analyzer's 2-hop BFS only accepts wires,
  0 Ω resistors, inductors, ferrites and jumpers as conducting edges, so it rejects the series Schottky and
  runs out of hops. Measured supply drop is 66 mV (see the DG419 section).
- **RS-001 — "VBAT_FUSED has no declared source"** — dismissed as a wiring fault; it is PWR_FLAG hygiene. The
  rail is sourced from J3 pin 1 through F1, and connector pins are passive so the analyzer sees no declared
  source. Adding a PWR_FLAG would silence it.
- **PM-002 connector courtyard overhangs (J1, J3–J6)** — almost certainly intentional for edge-mounted
  housings. Flagged for confirmation only.
- **CP-002 ×4 — "no opposite-layer copper under H1/H2/FID"** — expected for mounting holes and fiducials.
- **DRC `silk_*` warnings (19 total)** — cosmetic.
- **ERC `pin_to_pin` ×38** — all from Unspecified pin types on the connector and relay symbols. Library
  metadata, not wiring.

## Simulation verification

Run with ngspice-42 against the committed schematic. 6 subcircuits, **6 pass / 0 warn / 0 fail / 0 skip**.

| Subcircuit | Result |
|---|---|
| Voltage divider R2/R3 | Vout 1.65 V from 3.3 V, ratio 0.500, **0.00 % error** |
| Decoupling C1 (12V_PROT) | Z_min 174 mΩ; 509 mΩ @ 1 MHz; 4.83 Ω @ 100 kHz |
| Decoupling C2 (VIN) | Z_min 316 mΩ; 1.62 Ω @ 1 MHz; 15.9 Ω @ 100 kHz |
| Protection D9, D11 (12 V rail), D14 (3.3 V rail) | Present and correctly placed; clamping not measurable without manufacturer models |

Two caveats on reading these:

- **R2/R3 is not actually a divider.** The analyzer detects the topology and the simulation confirms the
  arithmetic, but the midpoint (`Net-(R2-Pad2)`) goes nowhere — the two 120 Ω parts are in series as a plain
  240 Ω pulldown on `SBU 1`. The 1.65 V figure is correct and irrelevant.
- **The protection "pass" results mean presence, not performance.** They use a generic diode model, so they
  say nothing about whether the SMAJ16A actually clamps where the design needs it to. The load-dump analysis
  earlier in this review remains inference-only.

The PDN numbers are the one genuinely new piece of information: 509 mΩ at 1 MHz on `12V_PROT` and 1.62 Ω on
`VIN` are high in absolute terms, but this board switches nothing faster than a relay and a CAN transceiver's
worth of edge rate, so there is no load that would care. C3 on `SW_VCC` was not picked up as a decoupling
group.

Nothing here changes the verdict or the blockers. The board simply has very little analog content to verify —
no regulators, filters, feedback networks or op-amp stages — so SPICE closes the gap in the review without
adding findings.
